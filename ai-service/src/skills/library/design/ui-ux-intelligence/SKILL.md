# UI/UX Intelligence (DevDrop original)

Independently written, general interface principles by site purpose. They shape which sections exist and how users move through them. They never override user content or the designSpec.

## Restaurant and cafe
Visitors want the menu, hours, location and a way to reserve or order. Put the menu and a reservation or contact action within one scroll of the top. Make address and opening hours tappable and easy to find. Photography leads when real photos exist.

## Hotel and stay
Visitors compare rooms and check dates. Lead with place and rooms, then amenities, location and a clear booking or enquiry path. Show room facts consistently (size, guests, features) in one repeatable card pattern.

## E-commerce
Support browse, filter, product detail and cart as one flow. Product cards show image, name and price consistently. Provide filter and sort that really work on the in-file data, a cart that updates quantities and totals, and an honest empty cart state.

## Portfolio and studio
The work is the page. Lead with projects or a sharp statement of what the person or studio does, then selected work with context (role, outcome), then about and contact. Give each project its own identity within a consistent frame.

## SaaS and landing
Explain what the product does in the first screen, show it (a mock interface or concrete example), map use cases to visitor goals, then offer one primary call to action repeated at natural decision points. Add pricing only when pricing facts were supplied.

## Blog and editorial
Optimize for reading: strong hierarchy, comfortable measure and line height, clear bylines and dates, easy discovery of related articles and categories. Chrome stays quiet so the writing carries the page.

## Event and education
Answer when, where, who and how to register or enrol. Present schedule or curriculum as scannable structured blocks. Keep the registration or enrol action persistent but unobtrusive.

## Dashboards and app screens
Establish information hierarchy first: key figures, then trends, then detail tables. Provide filters with visible applied state, sortable tables, and meaningful empty and error states.

## Forms
Label every field, validate inline with specific messages, keep the submit button available until a request starts, and confirm success clearly. Never present a form that silently does nothing: if there is no backend, say what happens (for example opens an email draft) or omit the form.

## Navigation
Keep top-level choices to roughly five to seven. Mark the current section. On small screens collapse into a menu that is keyboard operable and closes on selection.
